# ConstellationModule — constellations highlighted on the sky

A constellation drawn over the painted stars of the skybox: each star like a bright star photographed through a telescope (a small bright core, a soft halo, four thin diffraction spikes) and glowing lines of its figure. Nothing is
shown by default; a constellation picked in the debug window lights up with a reveal (star by star, line by line) and
the one shown before fades out. Only in `LobbyScene`, on each peer for itself (nothing is synced). Read the
[root AGENTS.md](../../../../AGENTS.md) first (game, rules, module map). Change this module's description here, not in
the root file.

## Assembly
- `Features.ConstellationModule.Data` (layer 1; no references): `ConstellationRevealModel`, which catalog constellation
  this peer's sky shows and how far its reveal is, plus the one fading out after a switch; local and unsynced, bound in
  `DataInstaller`.
- `Features.ConstellationModule` (layer 2; references: ConstellationModule.Data, DebugModule.Contracts, Zenject): the
  constellation data and catalog, the sky frame and look configurations, the mesh builder, the reveal timeline and fade
  rule, the systems and the views; installed by `ConstellationModuleInstaller` in `LobbySceneInstaller` (the lobby's
  scene context), so its systems and debug buttons live only while the lobby is loaded. The three configurations are in
  the Addressables `Configurations` group and bound in `ConfigurationInstaller`. Tests:
  `Features.ConstellationModule.Editor.Tests`.

## Key places
| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Constellation as data | `ConstellationDefinition` (`GameResources/Constellations/`): display name (the debug button's label), stars (name, RA and Dec in degrees, J2000, V magnitude) and the lines of the usual figure as pairs of star indices, **in reveal order** (a line is drawn from its first star to its second). At most 32 stars and 32 lines (the shader's step array; the mesh builder throws past it). `ConstellationCatalog_Default` (`GameResources/Configurations`) lists the constellations the sky can show: a new constellation is a new asset and an entry in the catalog, no code. Orion: Betelgeuse, Bellatrix, Meissa, Mintaka, Alnilam, Alnitak, Saiph, Rigel; lines head -> shoulders -> body -> belt -> legs: Meissa-Betelgeuse, Meissa-Bellatrix, Betelgeuse-Alnitak, Bellatrix-Mintaka, Mintaka-Alnilam, Alnilam-Alnitak, Alnitak-Saiph, Mintaka-Rigel. Big Dipper (`BigDipper.asset`): Dubhe, Merak, Phecda, Megrez, Alioth, Mizar, Alkaid; lines handle end -> bowl, bowl closed: Alkaid-Mizar, Mizar-Alioth, Alioth-Megrez, Megrez-Dubhe, Dubhe-Merak, Merak-Phecda, Phecda-Megrez. Positions and magnitudes from AstroTracker's `ObjectCatalog/stars.txt` (Rigel 0.18, Betelgeuse 0.45, Bellatrix 1.64, Alnilam 1.69, Alnitak 1.74, Saiph 2.07, Mintaka 2.25, Meissa 3.39; Alioth 1.76, Dubhe 1.81, Alkaid 1.85, Mizar 2.23, Merak 2.34, Phecda 2.41, Megrez 3.32). `ConstellationStar.CelestialDirection` is the catalogue position as a unit vector in Unity's axis order. |
| Where the painted sky lies | `CelestialSkyFrameConfiguration` (`GameResources/Configurations/CelestialSkyFrameConfiguration_LobbySky.asset`, for `StarSkybox04.mat`, `_Rotation` 0): a catalogue position (RA a, Dec d) is written in Unity's axis order (x = cos d cos a, y = sin d, z = cos d sin a: the swap that turns the right-handed celestial frame into Unity's left-handed one), then turned by `Euler(0, -90, 0)`. So RA 0 / Dec 0 is world +Z, RA 90 is -X, the north pole is +Y: a camera with yaw = -RA and pitch = -Dec looks at (RA, Dec); the celestial equator is the lobby's horizon. Found on 2026-11-27 by plate solving 1024 px, 60 deg renders of the skybox with AstroTracker's solver (8 frames, 38-51 matched stars each, fit RMS 26" = 0.11 px); Betelgeuse, Rigel, Bellatrix and Sirius land within 0.1 px of the painted stars. Another skybox, or a skybox `_Rotation`, needs its own frame. |
| Look | `ConstellationHighlightConfiguration_Default`, all sizes in degrees for a star of the reference magnitude (1.0). Magnitude: light = 10^(-0.4 (m - 1)); size factor = light^size exponent and brightness factor = light^brightness exponent, each clamped to 0.35..2. Since 2026-11-27 both ranges start at 1 (were 0.35): a star fainter than the reference magnitude is drawn at the reference look instead of shrinking to a dot, and only the brighter ones grow (Rigel x1.46, Betelgeuse x1.29, every other Orion star x1), so the big painted stars are not outgrown by their highlight. Star, three layers in one quad, tint (0.95, 0.97, 1): core = Gaussian, radius 0.04, intensity 8 (HDR, so the scene's Bloom, threshold 1, gives a tight glow); halo = (1 - r / 0.55)^3, intensity 0.3; spikes = a cross of four, length 1.0, width 0.015 at the centre, intensity 1.3, width and brightness taper to zero at the tip (narrower than a pixel they keep a pixel's width and dim instead), turned 15 deg clockwise from an upright '+'; twinkle = the whole star flickers by 30 % (three sines at 3, 7.1 and 15.4 rad/s, so no visible rhythm; the halo and spikes swing about twice as much as the core), a phase per star (0 turns it off). Line: a strip 3 px wide on screen, tint (0.45, 0.62, 1), kept faint on purpose (seen only when one looks for it): a thin Gaussian core (intensity 0.06, below Bloom's threshold, so no bloom halo) over a soft glow (0.008) filling the strip, fading in over 0.25 deg at each end; it stops 0.3 deg x the star's size factor short of each star, so the belt keeps its lines. |
| Reveal | `ConstellationRevealTimelineFactory` lays a figure's reveal out from its line order: a line's first star lights up if not lit yet (fade-in), a pause, the line grows from its first star to its second (time = angle / speed), its second star lights up if not lit yet, a pause; a line that closes on a lit star (Orion's belt end, the Dipper's bowl) is followed by the pause alone; stars on no line light up after the last line. `ConstellationRevealTimeline` answers each star's light (0..1, eased in with a flash past 1 halfway: smoothstep + overshoot x sin; exactly 1 once lit) and each line's drawn share (0..1) at a reveal time. Timing in `ConstellationHighlightConfiguration` ("Reveal"): star fade-in 0.3 s, flash overshoot 0.5, lines 15 deg/s, pause 0.1 s, whole-figure fade-out 0.8 s, switch delay 0.3 s; Orion takes about 6.3 s, the Big Dipper about 5.8 s. `ConstellationFadeRule`: on a switch the old figure fades out as a whole (smoothstep) and the new reveal starts 0.3 s later (a cross-fade); picking the shown figure again waits for the whole fade-out, then replays; the first pick starts at once. `ConstellationRevealSystem` (`ITickable`) advances the model's reveal and fade clocks by the scaled frame time, drops the faded figure when its fade is over, and clears the model when the lobby unloads, so a lobby always starts with an empty sky. |
| Debug | `ConstellationDebugSystem` (editor / development builds, debug window section "Constellations"): one button per catalog constellation (its display name; reveals it, or replays it when shown) and "Hide" (fades the shown one out; greyed out when nothing is shown). Local only, any player. |
| Drawing | `ConstellationHighlightView` (on the `ConstellationHighlight` object in `LobbyScene`, injected by the lobby's scene context) draws what `ConstellationRevealModel` says on two child figures: `ShownFigure` (the shown constellation at its reveal time) and `FadingFigure` (the one switched away from, frozen at the reveal it had reached, times the fade-out alpha), so two figures can cross-fade. Each child has a `ConstellationFigureView` with its `MeshFilter` and `MeshRenderer` (disabled in the scene until a constellation is shown): it builds the mesh with `ConstellationMeshBuilder` when its constellation changes, hands the star / line look to the shader per submesh (property blocks; `_IsLine` picks the look) and every frame the per-step progress (`_StepProgress[32]`: each star's light on the star submesh, each line's drawn share on the line submesh) and `_FigureAlpha`. Every vertex carries its reveal step in UV1.w (the star's or line's index); the vertex shader picks its progress from the array, a star is multiplied by its light, a line is cut at its drawn share with a soft head as long as the end fade (the head runs past the end once drawn, so a drawn line is not dimmed), and the whole figure by `_FigureAlpha`. A fully revealed figure is pixel-identical to the figure before the reveal existed (checked 2026-11-27 against the old shader, twinkle off). There is no edit-mode preview any more: the Scene view shows nothing. Every vertex is a world direction: a star is a quad of four directions around it, its axes the spike axes (screen right / up for a camera looking at the star with world up on top, turned by the spike angle), UV0 = shape position in degrees, brightness, twinkle phase; a line is a quad of its two end directions, UV1 = the other end's direction, and the vertex shader widens it sideways in pixels (a great circle projects to a straight line, so the strip stays on the stars). `ConstellationAtInfinity.shader` (`GameResources/Shaders`, material `GameResources/Materials/ConstellationHighlight.mat`) projects a direction with the view-projection matrix and w = 0, so the camera's position is ignored like the skybox's, and puts the depth just inside the far plane: the figure never slides against the stars when the player walks or flies, any world geometry hides it, it is unlit, unfogged, added onto the sky (transparent queue, `Blend One One`). The mesh has huge bounds so it is never frustum-culled. Local only: nothing is networked. |

## Known limits
- Only `LobbyScene` has the highlight; `GameScene` uses the same skybox and frame but has no highlight object yet.
- The stars are drawn over the painted stars, not from them: the size follows the catalogue magnitude, but the colour is one tint for all (Betelgeuse looks orange only through its painted star).
- The spikes follow world up, not the screen: when the camera rolls (or looks near a pole) they turn with the sky.
- A figure view reads the look configuration on awake: a change in the asset shows after the lobby is loaded again (the reveal timings are read on every pick).
- Switching twice within the fade-out drops the older fading figure at once (only one figure fades at a time).
- Checked in editor renders (batchmode camera renders of `LobbyScene` with the view wired by hand) and EditMode tests, not in Play mode with the real debug window.
