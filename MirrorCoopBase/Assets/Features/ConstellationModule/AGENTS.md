# ConstellationModule — constellations highlighted on the sky

A constellation drawn over the painted stars of the skybox: each star like a bright star photographed through a telescope (a small bright core, a soft halo, four thin diffraction spikes) and glowing lines of its figure. Nothing is
shown by default; a constellation the host picks (debug window, later missions), or all of them at once ("Show all"),
lights up on every player's sky with a reveal (star by star, line by line) and the one shown before fades out. Only in `LobbyScene`; the shown constellation is
a shared synced model written by the server, and every peer runs the animation itself from it in network time. Read the
[root AGENTS.md](../../../../AGENTS.md) first (game, rules, module map). Change this module's description here, not in
the root file.

## Assembly
- `Features.ConstellationModule.Data` (layer 1; references: NetworkModel, Mirror, Zenject): the generated
  `ConstellationSky` synced model (`Data/Generated`, definition
  `NetworkModelModule/GameResources/Definitions/ConstellationSky.asset`, Shared, atomic), `IConstellationSkyWriter` and
  `ConstellationSkyBindingModel` (the server's writer), `ConstellationSkyIds` (reserved sky ids: `ALL_CONSTELLATIONS`
  = `*`, the whole catalog), and `ConstellationRevealModel`, which catalog constellation this peer's sky draws (or
  `ALL_CONSTELLATIONS` = -2, every one) and how far its reveal is, plus the one fading out after a switch (local, filled from the synced
  model); all bound in `DataInstaller` (the synced one through `ConstellationSkyModelInstaller`).
- `Features.ConstellationModule` (layer 2; references: ConstellationModule.Data, DebugModule.Contracts, Zenject,
  Mirror, NetworkModel): the service and the sky's network bridge, the
  constellation data and catalog, the sky frame and look configurations, the mesh builder, the reveal timeline and fade
  rule, the systems and the views; installed by `ConstellationModuleInstaller` in `LobbySceneInstaller` (the lobby's
  scene context), so its systems and debug buttons live only while the lobby is loaded. The three configurations are in
  the Addressables `Configurations` group and bound in `ConfigurationInstaller`. Tests:
  `Features.ConstellationModule.Editor.Tests`.

## Key places
| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Constellation as data | `ConstellationDefinition` (`GameResources/Constellations/`): a stable id (`orion`, `leo`; the synced sky names a constellation by it, found with `ConstellationCatalog.TryFindIndex`), display name (the debug button's label), stars (name, RA and Dec in degrees, J2000, V magnitude) and the lines of the usual figure as pairs of star indices, **in reveal order** (a line is drawn from its first star to its second). The whole catalog together has at most 256 stars and 256 lines (the shader's step array, since "Show all" draws every figure in one mesh; the mesh builder throws past it, `ConstellationCatalogGuardTests` checks it; 124 stars and 119 lines on 2026-11-27). `ConstellationCatalog_Default` (`GameResources/Configurations`) lists the constellations the sky can show: a new constellation is a new asset and an entry in the catalog, no code (all 17 and the steps to add one: "Constellations" below). Orion: Betelgeuse, Bellatrix, Meissa, Mintaka, Alnilam, Alnitak, Saiph, Rigel; lines head -> shoulders -> body -> belt -> legs: Meissa-Betelgeuse, Meissa-Bellatrix, Betelgeuse-Alnitak, Bellatrix-Mintaka, Mintaka-Alnilam, Alnilam-Alnitak, Alnitak-Saiph, Mintaka-Rigel. Positions and magnitudes from AstroTracker's `ObjectCatalog/stars.txt` (Rigel 0.18, Betelgeuse 0.45, Bellatrix 1.64, Alnilam 1.69, Alnitak 1.74, Saiph 2.07, Mintaka 2.25, Meissa 3.39). `ConstellationStar.CelestialDirection` is the catalogue position as a unit vector in Unity's axis order. |
| Where the painted sky lies | `CelestialSkyFrameConfiguration` (`GameResources/Configurations/CelestialSkyFrameConfiguration_LobbySky.asset`, for `StarSkybox04.mat`, `_Rotation` 0): a catalogue position (RA a, Dec d) is written in Unity's axis order (x = cos d cos a, y = sin d, z = cos d sin a: the swap that turns the right-handed celestial frame into Unity's left-handed one), then turned by `Euler(0, -90, 0)`. So RA 0 / Dec 0 is world +Z, RA 90 is -X, the north pole is +Y: a camera with yaw = -RA and pitch = -Dec looks at (RA, Dec); the celestial equator is the lobby's horizon. Found on 2026-11-27 by plate solving 1024 px, 60 deg renders of the skybox with AstroTracker's solver (8 frames, 38-51 matched stars each, fit RMS 26" = 0.11 px); Betelgeuse, Rigel, Bellatrix and Sirius land within 0.1 px of the painted stars. Another skybox, or a skybox `_Rotation`, needs its own frame. |
| Look | `ConstellationHighlightConfiguration_Default`, all sizes in degrees for a star of the reference magnitude (1.0). Magnitude: light = 10^(-0.4 (m - 1)); size factor = light^size exponent and brightness factor = light^brightness exponent, each clamped to 0.35..2. Since 2026-11-27 both ranges start at 1 (were 0.35): a star fainter than the reference magnitude is drawn at the reference look instead of shrinking to a dot, and only the brighter ones grow (Rigel x1.46, Betelgeuse x1.29, every other Orion star x1), so the big painted stars are not outgrown by their highlight. Star, three layers in one quad, tint (0.95, 0.97, 1): core = Gaussian, radius 0.04, intensity 8 (HDR, so the scene's Bloom, threshold 1, gives a tight glow); halo = (1 - r / 0.55)^3, intensity 0.3; spikes = a cross of four, length 1.0, width 0.015 at the centre, intensity 1.3, width and brightness taper to zero at the tip (narrower than a pixel they keep a pixel's width and dim instead), turned 15 deg clockwise from an upright '+'; twinkle = the whole star flickers by 30 % (three sines at 3, 7.1 and 15.4 rad/s, so no visible rhythm; the halo and spikes swing about twice as much as the core), a phase per star (0 turns it off). Line: a strip 3 px wide on screen, tint (0.45, 0.62, 1), kept faint on purpose (seen only when one looks for it): a thin Gaussian core (intensity 0.06, below Bloom's threshold, so no bloom halo) over a soft glow (0.008) filling the strip, fading in over 0.25 deg at each end; it stops 0.3 deg x the star's size factor short of each star, so the belt keeps its lines. |
| Reveal | `ConstellationRevealTimelineFactory` lays a figure's reveal out from its line order: a line's first star lights up if not lit yet (fade-in), a pause, the line grows from its first star to its second (time = angle / speed), its second star lights up if not lit yet, a pause; a line that closes on a lit star (Orion's belt end, Pegasus' Great Square) is followed by the pause alone; stars on no line light up after the last line. `ConstellationRevealTimeline` answers each star's light (0..1, eased in with a flash past 1 halfway: smoothstep + overshoot x sin; exactly 1 once lit) and each line's drawn share (0..1) at a reveal time. Timing in `ConstellationHighlightConfiguration` ("Reveal"): star fade-in 0.3 s, flash overshoot 0.5, lines 15 deg/s, pause 0.1 s, whole-figure fade-out 0.8 s, switch delay 0.3 s; Orion takes about 6.3 s. `ConstellationFadeRule`: on a switch the old figure fades out as a whole (smoothstep) and the new reveal starts 0.3 s later (a cross-fade); picking the shown figure again waits for the whole fade-out, then replays; the first pick starts at once. `ConstellationRevealSystem` (`IInitializable`, `ITickable`) fills `ConstellationRevealModel` from the synced sky (see "Network flow"), sets its clock to `NetworkTime.time` every frame (the reveal seconds are `NetworkTime.time - RevealStartTime`, so every peer is in step; on the host this is the unscaled local time, the same as the scaled frame time used before at time scale 1), drops the faded figure when its fade is over, and clears the model when the lobby unloads or the sky's bridge goes away, so a lobby always starts with an empty sky. |
| Network flow | `IConstellationService` (`ConstellationService`, server only; missions will call it too): `ServerShow(id)` (an id outside the catalog throws), `ServerShowAll()` (writes the reserved id `*`: every catalog figure reveals from the same start, each on its own timeline, so they finish at different times) and `ServerHide()` (nothing shown: no write; fades all of them when all are shown). "All" is one more sky id for the state rule: picking one while all are shown is a switch (all fade, the one starts 0.3 s later), "Show all" while one is shown is a switch too, "Show all" while all are shown is a replay (waits the whole fade). It is the one place that writes the synced `ConstellationSky` model, through `ConstellationSkyBindingModel.Writer`: `ConstellationSkySync` (`Scripts/Network`, a root scene object with a `NetworkIdentity` in `LobbyScene`, built on the generated `ConstellationSkyBridge`) binds itself as the writer on `OnStartServer`; outside the lobby or on a pure client there is no writer and the service throws. `ConstellationSkyStateRule` makes the state: `ConstellationId` (empty = nothing shown), `RevealStartTime` (network time the reveal begins: the pick time plus the fade rule's delay, so a switch's cross-fade and a replay's wait are in the state) and `ChangeTime` (network time of the last pick or hide: the figure shown before fades out from it on every peer). Each peer applies a change to its `ConstellationRevealModel`: the figure it showed fades from the reveal it had at `ChangeTime`; the same reveal told twice changes nothing. |
| Late join / rejoin | A joiner gets the full state on spawn (`OnStartClient`) and derives the reveal from network time: a fully shown figure appears fully at once (no replay), a reveal under way is seen from where it is, a hidden sky stays empty; a fade-out in progress at the join is not replayed (the joiner had no figure to fade). The sky lives on the lobby's scene object, so a new lobby (back from a run) starts empty. |
| Debug | `ConstellationDebugSystem` (editor / development builds, debug window section "Constellations"): "Show all" (`IConstellationService.ServerShowAll()`), then one button per catalog constellation (its display name; `ServerShow(id)`: reveals it for everyone, or replays it when shown) and "Hide" (`ServerHide`: fades what is shown out for everyone; greyed out when nothing is shown). Host only: the buttons are always registered (the lobby scene is loaded *before* the host starts its server, so `NetworkServer.active` is still false in `Initialize`) and are greyed out with `DebugActionReasons.HOST_ONLY` while `NetworkServer.active` is false, like the other host-only debug actions. |
| Drawing | `ConstellationHighlightView` (on the `ConstellationHighlight` object in `LobbyScene`, injected by the lobby's scene context) draws what `ConstellationRevealModel` says on two child figures: `ShownFigure` (the shown constellation, or every catalog constellation in catalog order in one mesh, at its reveal time) and `FadingFigure` (the one switched away from, frozen at the reveal it had reached, times the fade-out alpha), so two figures can cross-fade. Each child has a `ConstellationFigureView` with its `MeshFilter` and `MeshRenderer` (disabled in the scene until a constellation is shown): it builds one mesh of all its figures with `ConstellationMeshBuilder` when its constellations change (still two submeshes, so "Show all" is the same draw calls as one figure), hands the star / line look to the shader per submesh (property blocks; `_IsLine` picks the look) and every frame the per-step progress (`_StepProgress[256]`: each star's light on the star submesh, each line's drawn share on the line submesh, every figure's steps after the figures before it) and `_FigureAlpha`. Every vertex carries its reveal step in UV1.w (the star's or line's index, counted on from the figures before it; a figure's vertices and twinkle phases are the same alone or with others, so its look is unchanged); the vertex shader picks its progress from the array, a star is multiplied by its light, a line is cut at its drawn share with a soft head as long as the end fade (the head runs past the end once drawn, so a drawn line is not dimmed), and the whole figure by `_FigureAlpha`. A fully revealed figure is pixel-identical to the figure before the reveal existed (checked 2026-11-27 against the old shader, twinkle off). There is no edit-mode preview any more: the Scene view shows nothing. Every vertex is a world direction: a star is a quad of four directions around it, its axes the spike axes (screen right / up for a camera looking at the star with world up on top, turned by the spike angle), UV0 = shape position in degrees, brightness, twinkle phase; a line is a quad of its two end directions, UV1 = the other end's direction, and the vertex shader widens it sideways in pixels (a great circle projects to a straight line, so the strip stays on the stars). `ConstellationAtInfinity.shader` (`GameResources/Shaders`, material `GameResources/Materials/ConstellationHighlight.mat`) projects a direction with the view-projection matrix and w = 0, so the camera's position is ignored like the skybox's, and puts the depth just inside the far plane: the figure never slides against the stars when the player walks or flies, any world geometry hides it, it is unlit, unfogged, added onto the sky (transparent queue, `Blend One One`). The mesh has huge bounds so it is never frustum-culled. The drawing is local; what it draws comes from the synced sky. |

## Constellations
In the lobby the celestial equator lies on the horizon (RA 0 / Dec 0 at world +Z, RA 90 at -X, north pole up), so a
star's declination is its height above the horizon. Players look around, not up: the catalog keeps only figures in a
band around the horizon, **every star at declination -20 to +32 deg**, and fills it all the way round.

`ConstellationCatalog_Default` in order of right ascension (the centre of the figure, 0 -> 360 deg), so the debug
window's buttons go round the horizon like the sky. Stars by IAU name from AstroTracker's `ObjectCatalog/stars.txt`;
stars not named there (marked *) from its Tycho-2 catalogue (`StarCatalog/tycho2.bytes`, the bright star in a cone
around the known position; positions at epoch 2026.8 in the J2000 frame, the proper motion since 2000 is under 0.2 px
of the check render). Lines in reveal order. "Dec" is the lowest and highest star. "Worst" is the largest distance of a
figure star to its painted star in a sky-only render (1024 px, 60 deg, 232.6"/px) centred on the figure, measured
2026-11-27; a star moved by 0.25 deg reads about 4 px there.

Left out by the band rule (2026-11-27): Big Dipper, Little Dipper, Cassiopeia, Cygnus, Lyra (all high in the north),

stars left).

Adding a constellation (data only, no code):
1. Check the band first: every star of the figure at declination -20 to +32 deg. A classic figure that pokes out may
   be trimmed to its in-band part if that still reads as the figure (say what was trimmed in the row), otherwise it is
   left out.
2. Create a `ConstellationDefinition` asset in `GameResources/Constellations/` (Create menu, or copy an asset and give
   the copy a new GUID in Unity): a unique lower-case id (it is what the synced sky sends; never change a shipped one),
   the English display name (the debug button's label), roughly 4-12 stars of the usual stick figure with no faint
   filler (the whole catalog at most 256 stars and 256 lines), each with name, J2000 RA / Dec in degrees and V magnitude (from
   `stars.txt`, else Tycho-2), and the lines as star index pairs in reveal order (one continuous walk where possible,
   then the branches; a line is drawn from its first star).
3. Add it to `_constellations` of `ConstellationCatalog_Default` at its place by right ascension (it becomes a debug
   button; `ConstellationCatalogGuardTests` checks that the id is set and unique).
4. Check that every star sits on its painted star: render the lobby sky (sky only, 1024 px, 60 deg) centred on the
   figure, project each star through `CelestialSkyFrameConfiguration` and measure the distance to the painted star's
   centroid; more than about 2 px means a wrong star or position (a star missing from the skybox, like Mira, reads as
   no star at all). Add the row above.

## Known limits
- Only `LobbyScene` has the highlight; `GameScene` uses the same skybox and frame but has no highlight object yet.
- The stars are drawn over the painted stars, not from them: the size follows the catalogue magnitude, but the colour is one tint for all (Betelgeuse looks orange only through its painted star).
- The spikes follow world up, not the screen: when the camera rolls (or looks near a pole) they turn with the sky.
- A figure view reads the look configuration on awake: a change in the asset shows after the lobby is loaded again (the reveal timings are read on every pick).
- Switching twice within the fade-out drops the older fading figure at once (only one figure fades at a time).
- "Show all" was checked in batchmode renders (2026-11-27: frames at 0.5 / 2 / 5 s and fully revealed from three directions, wide views, a hide and a switch to Orion) and EditMode tests, not with the real debug window in Play mode nor across two processes.
- Checked in editor renders (batchmode camera renders of `LobbyScene` with the view wired by hand) and EditMode tests, not in Play mode with the real debug window.
- The network sync (2026-11-27) is covered by EditMode tests only: a two-process check (host + client build) did not get past the host's bootstrap in batchmode PlayMode, so host, client and late join were not seen running.
- A joiner whose network clock is not synced yet (the first moments after connecting) sees nothing until it is; the figure then appears at its current reveal.
