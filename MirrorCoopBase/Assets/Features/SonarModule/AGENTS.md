# SonarModule — the navigator's sonar console

The navigator's sonar console on the radar module. Read the [root AGENTS.md](../../../../AGENTS.md) first (game, rules, module map). Change this module's description here, not in the root file.

## Assembly
- `Features.SonarModule.Contracts` (layer 1; no references): `SonarModule/Contracts/`: `ISonarContactSource`, `SonarContact`, `SonarContactKind`, `ISonarCondition`, so a module adds its own sonar contacts or says whether the radar works without referencing the sonar.
- `Features.SonarModule.Data` (layer 1; references: SonarModule.Contracts, NetworkModel, Mirror, Zenject): the generated `SonarConsole` model with `SonarMark`, and the sonar's local models.
- `Features.SonarModule` (layer 6; references: SonarModule.Contracts, SonarModule.Data, Ship, Grab, Camera, CharacterMovable, Mvp, PlayerLife, FlightFieldModule.Contracts, GameCoreModule.Contracts, GameFlow, NetworkModel, Mirror, Zenject, Input, UnityEngine.UI, DebugModule.Contracts): the navigator's sonar console; its own module rather than more radar code in the ship module, because it needs the UI, the player's life and the field contracts the ship must not depend on, and the ship stays unaware of it.

## Key places
| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Sonar (navigator) | `Assets/Features/SonarModule/` (`Contracts`, `Data`, `Scripts`). The radar module (`RadarModule.asset`, slot `radar`) is a **console**: its slot view `RadarItemView` is a pedestal with a screen, and `ShipPlatform/RadarSocket/SonarConsole` (trigger box on the screen, layer 7) carries `SonarConsoleInteractable`. The console is active only while a module stands in the socket and the ship flies (`ShipRunPhase.IsFlight()`: Takeoff / Cruise / Landing), so at a station E on the module still takes it off. E sends the use command; the server (`SonarNavigatorSystem`, `SonarConsoleRule`) seats **one navigator by `PlayerKey`** in the synced Shared `SonarConsole` model (`NavigatorKey`; bridge `SonarConsoleSync` on `RadarSocket`, which also binds the console in `SonarConsoleBindingModel`) and opens the console on that player's peer (`TargetOpenConsole` -> `SonarScopeModel.Open`). `SonarWindowSystem` then opens the MVP `SonarWindow` (`SonarScopeView` / `SonarScopePresenter`, prefab `SonarModule/GameResources/Prefabs/SonarWindow`, Addressables `Windows`), blocks walking and aim (`PlayerControlBlockModel`) and frees the cursor; Esc, E or the Leave button close it, `SonarConsoleReachSystem` closes it when the player dies, walks farther than `ConsoleKeepOpenMeters` or the server gave the seat away; closing sends `CmdLeave`; the server also frees the seat on disconnect, distance or landing. **Ping game** (`SonarScopeSystem`, numbers in `SonarConfiguration_Default`, Addressables `Configurations`): Space or the Ping button (cooldown `PingCooldownSeconds`) takes the contacts within range from this peer's own data, a wave runs from the ship to the edge in `PingWaveSeconds` and lights each contact as it passes, an echo fades over `EchoFadeSeconds`; top view around the ship, nose up, `RangeRingCount` (4) rings with their distances (`SonarScopeLayout`). The range is the ship's `SonarRange` stat (synced in `ShipStats`; the radar module adds 1 000 m, never below `MinRangeMeters`, 1 000 m: the crew sees only about 150-260 m by eye through the [space haze](../SpaceHazeModule/AGENTS.md), so the navigator leads). **The scope says what it shows:** a legend of every kind of `SonarConfiguration.Kinds` (icon, colour, name; `SonarLegendRow`), the hint line (how to ping and mark), the ship's heading ("HEADING 045°") with north ("N") on the scope's edge, and without a ping the next station's bearing (an arrow on the edge, inside when in range) and distance (`SonarBearingService` -> `SonarScopeBearings` in the frame; none at a station or with a broken radar); an echo is as big as its contact (its radius against the range, never smaller than the kind's icon), the biggest drawn first so a small echo over a giant still takes the click (`SonarBlipSizeComparer`). The ping reads every contact of the 1 000 m disc in one frame from the peer queries: measured in the editor 2.8-9 ms per ping (86-161 contacts; it was about a ninth of that at 300 m); slicing it into tiles costs more in total because the field's rock query scans a square around a box's 3D bounding sphere. **Contacts** come only through `ISonarContactSource` (Contracts), all bound in `SonarModuleInstaller` and read as one by `SonarContactService`: `RockSonarContactSource` (every giant (`FieldRockSize.Large`) and every debris rock at least `BigRockMinDiameterMeters` (40 m) across of `IPeerFieldRockQuery`, the published field on every peer, is a contact `rock:c:..` / `rock:d:..` of the `LargeRockKind` / `BigRockKind`; smaller rocks count per `DebrisCellMeters` (100 m) world cell, `DebrisMinRocks` (3) make one `debris:x:z` contact), `StationSonarContactSource` (`station`, the run's `TransitDestination`, in flight). Field ore is not a contact and `BugSonarContactSource` (BugModule, bound in its own installer: every bug of the peer's last agent batch is a `bug:<id>` contact of `SonarKind_Bug`, "Bug" green, not markable). `ParcelSonarContactSource` (ParcelModule, its own installer: every stolen parcel, carried, drifting or pulled, is a `parcel:<netId>` contact of `SonarKind_StolenParcel`, "Stolen parcel" orange, markable). Field ore is not a contact and has no legend row (it would only clutter the scope; the balloon contacts and their `SonarKind_Target*` kinds and tiers were removed with the balloons on 2026-10-28). A kind is a `SonarContactKind` asset (id, colour, icon, pixels, mark seconds, markable, shown on the edge beyond range: `SonarKind_Station` blue square, `SonarKind_LargeRock` "Giant rock" see-through red disc, `SonarKind_BigRock` orange diamond, `SonarKind_Debris` "Small rocks" sand dots), never a branch. **Marks:** a click on an echo -> `CmdMark` -> `SonarMarkSystem` with `SonarMarkRule` (`CreateMark` builds the mark; the sender is the navigator, the contact exists now and is markable, at most `MarkLimit` marks; marking a marked contact renews it) -> `SonarMark` (contact id, kind id, position over the object's top, `ExpiresAt` in network time) in the synced `Marks` list, so a late joiner gets the active marks; the server drops a mark when its time is up or its contact is gone (a broken rock). Every peer: `SonarMarkerSystem` lays the marks out on its screen (`SonarMarkerProjection`: over the object, else on the screen edge less `MarkerEdgeMarginPixels` with an arrow) into `SonarMarkerScreenModel`, and `SonarMarkersView` in `GameHudWindow` draws icon, glow, distance and arrow at a constant size. **Own station marker (debug):** the debug window's Ship → "Show station: off / on" (`SonarStationMarkerDebugSystem`, editor / development builds only; the button registers again with the new label) sets the local, unsynced `SonarStationMarkerModel`; while it is on and the ship flies, `SonarStationMarkerSource` hands `SonarMarkerSystem` the station from `IStationSonarContactSource` (`StationSonarContactSource`, the synced run's `TransitDestination`), laid out like a crew mark but only on this peer's screen, so a pilot playing solo sees where to fly. Off by default; off or at a station nothing is shown. **Broken radar:** `RadarSonarCondition` (`ISonarCondition`) asks `IShipModuleCondition` about the console's socket (`SonarConsoleSync._socket`, bound in `SonarConsoleBindingModel`): a broken radar still opens and pings, but its wave finds nothing and the window shows "NO SIGNAL: the radar needs repair" until a mechanic repairs it (with a tool in the hand the screen's collider offers the socket's repair, not the console). The old minimap (`ShipRadarService`, `ShipRadarScreen`, `ShipRadarCatalog`, the HUD radar) is gone |

## Known limits
- **The sonar reads the published field.** Its rock source uses the peer query (`IPeerFieldRockQuery`) over the live
  field model, so a navigator on a client sees the same contacts as one on the
  host, but only once the field is published (about a second into the takeoff on a loop 9 field); the server's checks
  keep the generated field.
